# Pitch Deck

Build pitch decks that move people — from "interesting" to "yes" — by demonstrating strategic thinking first, craft second.

## What You Do

You help structure and write pitch decks that communicate strategy and vision clearly, tell a compelling story, and end with a decisive call to action.

## Pitch Deck vs. Presentation Deck

- **Pitch deck**: Used to win something — a client, investment, an internal green light. Narrative-driven, shorter, designed to persuade.
- **Presentation deck**: Used to inform or update. Can be more comprehensive. Less optimised for persuasion.

## Pitch Types

- **Credentials / chemistry**: Early-stage — focused on fit, values, and ways of working
- **Strategic pitch**: Responding to a brief with a defined point of view and proposed approach
- **Speculative creative**: Showing creative work developed without a brief (use sparingly)

## Building the Pitch

1. **Audience and goal**: Define who will be in the room, what they care about individually, and what a successful outcome looks like. Research the audience specifically — most agencies present the same facts, the best tailor them.
2. **The hook**: Open with the client's challenge framed provocatively — not "we're excited to be here." This earns attention from slide one; it is not a title slide.
3. **Our point of view**: Your strategic take on the problem. This is where you differentiate from other pitches — a fresh perspective on the challenge, not just a restatement of it.
4. **The approach / solution**: How you would solve it. Specific phases, methods, and rationale for what you do differently and why it works.
5. **Proof selection**: Choose and shape 1–2 case studies that map most directly to the audience's industry or challenge — specific and relevant beats a broad portfolio tour.
6. **The team / why us**: Specific people working on this account, not generic agency credentials — the honest, specific reason this team is right for this challenge at this moment.
7. **The ask**: What you want. One clear, specific next step — not "any questions?"
8. **Review**: Check that every slide passes the "so what?" test — if you can't articulate why this slide moves the audience toward yes, cut or reframe it.

## Slide Principles

- One idea per slide
- Headlines should be statements, not labels — "We increased conversion by 34%" beats "Results"
- Visuals should add meaning, not decoration
- The deck should work as a leave-behind without narration — include speaker notes for live delivery
- Every slide should pass the "so what?" test — if you can't say why the audience should care, cut or reframe it

## Pitch Principles

- Know the audience: research who is in the room and what they care about individually
- Rehearse the Q&A as much as the presentation
- Leave time for a real conversation — pitches that feel like monologues rarely win
- Chemistry matters: clients hire people, not decks

## Anti-Patterns

- Agency history and credential overload — clients buy solutions, not histories
- Too many slides — a 40-slide pitch is not twice as convincing as a 20-slide pitch
- Vague language: "innovative", "holistic", "best-in-class" — replace with specifics
- No clear ask at the end
